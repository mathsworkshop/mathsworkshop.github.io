## Statement

Summarize the key points of continuity for several variables.

## Hint

Review methods and theorems.

## Solution

**Basic.** Polynomials and quotients of continuous functions (where denominator nonzero) are continuous.

**Continuity at a point.** Show $f(x,y)\to f(x_0,y_0)$; use bounding, polar coordinates, or the squeeze theorem.

**Discontinuity.** Exhibit two paths with different limits, or a path where the limit diverges or doesn't exist.

**Notable counterexamples.** $f(x,y)=\frac{x}{x+y}$ at origin, $f(x,y) = \sin(1/(x^2+y^2))$ at origin.


**Key theorems.** Continuous image of compact is compact; continuous on compact is uniformly continuous (Heine–Cantor); norm and projections are continuous.

## Statement

Show that $f(x,y)=x^2+3xy-y^2+2x-1$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

## Hint

Polynomials in several variables are continuous (sums and products of continuous coordinate projections).

## Solution

**Continuity of polynomials.** The functions $(x,y)\mapsto x$ and $(x,y)\mapsto y$ are continuous, and sums/products of continuous functions are continuous.

**Conclusion.** $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2$ by the composition rules.

## Statement

Show that $f(x,y)=\dfrac{x^3+y^3}{x^2+y^2}$ (with $f(0,0)=0$) is continuous at $(0,0)$.

## Hint

Use Polar coordinates. 

## Solution

**Polar coordinates.** For $(x,y) = (r\cos \theta, r \sin \theta)$, 
$$ |f(x,y)|  =r|\cos^3\theta+\sin^3\theta| \leq 2r = 2 \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \rightarrow 0 $$
as $(x,y) \rightarrow (0,0)$. 

**Conclusion.** $\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}f=0=f(0,0)$: $f$ is continuous at the origin.

## Statement

Show that the functions 
$$ f(x,y)=\dfrac{x}{x+y} \, \text{and} \, g(x,y)=\dfrac{xy}{x+y} $$ 
with $f(0,0)= g(0,0)=0$ are not continuous at the origin.

## Hint

Check the paths $y=ax$ and $y = ax + b x^2$.

## Solution

**Continuity of $f$.** On $y=ax$, $f(x,ax)=\frac{1}{1+a}$. As $x\to0$, this tends to $\frac{1}{1+a}$ for each $a \neq -1$. Hence $f$ is not continuous at the origin. 
\par
**Continuity of $g$.** On $y=ax$, $g(x,ax)=\frac{ax}{1+a} \rightarrow 0$ as $x\to0$ for any $a \neq -1$ (this does not mean $g$ is continuous!). Along $y=ax+bx^2$ with $a=-1$, $b\neq0$: 
$$ g(x,-x+bx^2)=\frac{x(-x+bx^2)}{bx^2}=\frac{-x+bx^2}{bx}=\frac{-1}{b}+x \rightarrow \frac{-1}{b} $$ 
as $x \rightarrow 0$. Limits differ along different paths; $g$ is not continuous at the origin.

**Remark.** The example of $g$ shows that checking limits along all straight lines through the origin is necessary but *not* sufficient for the existence of a two-variable limit.

## Statement

Show that $f(x,y)=\dfrac{x^2y}{x^4+y^2}$ (with $f(0,0)=0$) is not continuous at $(0,0)$, even though $f(x,ax)\to0$ for every $a$.

## Hint

Test the path $y=x^2$.

## Solution

**Radial limits.** $f(x,ax)=\frac{ax^3}{x^4+a^2x^2}=\frac{ax}{x^2+a^2}\to0$ for fixed $a$.

**Parabolic path.** $f(x,x^2)=\frac{x^4}{2x^4}=\frac12\not\to0$.

**Conclusion.** $f$ is not continuous at the origin despite radial limits being zero.

## Statement

Can $f(x,y)=\dfrac{\sin(xy)}{xy}$ be extended by continuity at $(0,0)$?

## Hint

Use $\sin t/t\to1$.

## Solution

**Limit.** Along $(x,y)\to(0,0)$ with $xy\neq0$, $\frac{\sin(xy)}{xy}\to1$.

**Extension.** Define $f(0,0)=1$; $f$ becomes continuous.

**Conclusion.** Yes, extension by $f(0,0)=1$ exists.

## Statement

Show that the continuous image of a compact set is compact, and deduce the existence of extrema.

## Hint

Use sequential compactness; continuous images of convergent sequences converge.

## Solution

**Image of compact.** Let $K$ compact and $f$ continuous. Given a sequence $(f(x_n))$ in $f(K)$, extract a subsequence $x_{n_k}\to x\in K$; then $f(x_{n_k})\to f(x)\in f(K)$. So $f(K)$ is sequentially compact.

**Extrema.** On a compact set, a continuous function attains max and min.

**Conclusion.** Continuous functions on compact sets attain their bounds.

@@@ continuity-heine-cantor.md

title: Heine–Cantor theorem
category: calculus/multivariable/continuity

date: 2026-11-03
---

## Statement

State and prove that a continuous function on a compact set is uniformly continuous.

## Hint

Argue by contradiction: assume $\exists\varepsilon>0$ such that $\forall\delta>0$ there are $x,y$ with $\|x-y\|<\delta$ and $\|f(x)-f(y)\|\ge\varepsilon$.

## Solution

**Contradiction.** If $f$ is not uniformly continuous, there is $\varepsilon>0$ and sequences $x_n,y_n$ in $K$ with $\|x_n-y_n\|\to0$ but $\|f(x_n)-f(y_n)\|\ge\varepsilon$.

**Subsequence.** By compactness, extract $x_{n_k}\to x\in K$. Then $y_{n_k}\to x$ too. Continuity gives $f(x_{n_k})\to f(x)$ and $f(y_{n_k})\to f(x)$, contradicting the gap.

**Conclusion.** $f$ is uniformly continuous on $K$.

## Statement

Show that the Euclidean norm $\|\cdot\|:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ is continuous.

## Hint

Use the reverse triangle inequality: $|\|x\|-\|y\||\le\|x-y\|$.

## Solution

**Inequality.** By the reverse triangle inequality, for any $x,y$: $|\|x\|-\|y\||\le\|x-y\|$.

**Conclusion.** $\|x_n\|\to\|x\|$ whenever $x_n\to x$: the norm is continuous.

## Statement

Show that the projection $\pi_1:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$, $(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\mapsto x_1$, is continuous.

## Hint

$|\pi_1(x)-\pi_1(y)|=|x_1-y_1|\le\|x-y\|_1$.

## Solution

**Bound.** $|\pi_1(x)-\pi_1(y)|\le\|x-y\|_1$.

**Conclusion.** $\pi_1$ is 1-Lipschitz, hence continuous.
