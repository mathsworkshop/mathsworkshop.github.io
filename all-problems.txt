## Statement

Show that the equation $x^3+x-1=0$ has exactly one real solution, and that it lies in $(0,1)$.

## Hint

Use the intermediate value theorem for existence and the derivative for uniqueness.

## Solution

Let $f(x)=x^3+x-1$. It is continuous, with $f(0)=-1<0$ and $f(1)=1>0$. By the intermediate value theorem, $f$ vanishes at some point of $(0,1)$.

Its derivative $f'(x)=3x^2+1$ is positive everywhere, so $f$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$. A strictly increasing function takes each value at most once, so the root is unique.

## Statement

Let $F=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^3:\ x+y+z=0\}$. Show that $F$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$, and find a basis and the dimension of $F$.

## Hint

Check that $F$ contains $0$ and is stable under sums and scalar multiples. Then solve for $z$.

## Solution

**Subspace.** $(0,0,0)\in F$. If $(x,y,z),(x',y',z')\in F$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$, then
$$(x+\lambda x')+(y+\lambda y')+(z+\lambda z')=(x+y+z)+\lambda(x'+y'+z')=0,$$
so $(x,y,z)+\lambda(x',y',z')\in F$.

**Basis.** A vector of $F$ has $z=-x-y$, so
$$(x,y,z)=x\,(1,0,-1)+y\,(0,1,-1).$$
The vectors $(1,0,-1)$ and $(0,1,-1)$ span $F$ and are linearly independent (neither is a multiple of the other). They form a basis, and $\dim F=2$.

date: 2026-10-31

## Statement

Let $u_0=0$ and $u_{n+1}=\sqrt{2+u_n}$. Show that $(u_n)$ converges and find its limit.

## Hint

Show by induction that $0\le u_n<2$, then that $(u_n)$ is increasing.

## Solution

**Bounded.** We show $0\le u_n<2$ by induction. It holds for $n=0$. If $0\le u_n<2$, then $2\le2+u_n<4$, so $\sqrt2\le u_{n+1}<2$.

**Increasing.** Since all terms are non-negative,
$$u_{n+1}^2-u_n^2=2+u_n-u_n^2=(2-u_n)(1+u_n)>0,$$
so $u_{n+1}>u_n$.

**Limit.** An increasing sequence bounded above converges, say to $L$ with $0\le L\le2$. Passing to the limit in $u_{n+1}=\sqrt{2+u_n}$ gives $L=\sqrt{2+L}$, so $L^2-L-2=0$, that is $(L-2)(L+1)=0$. Since $L\ge0$, $L=2$.

## Statement

Show that $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}x\,e^{-x}\,dx$ converges and compute its value.

## Hint

Find an antiderivative by parts, then let the upper bound go to infinity.

## Solution

An antiderivative of $xe^{-x}$ is $F(x)=-(x+1)e^{-x}$, because
$$F'(x)=-e^{-x}+(x+1)e^{-x}=xe^{-x}.$$

For $A>0$,
$$\int_0^Axe^{-x}\,dx=F(A)-F(0)=1-(A+1)e^{-A}.$$

As $A\to+\infty$, $(A+1)e^{-A}\to0$ (exponential beats polynomial). So the integral converges and equals $1$.

## Statement

Compute the area of the triangle with vertices $A(1,2)$, $B(5,3)$ and $C(2,7)$.

## Hint

The area is half the absolute value of the determinant of $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$.

## Solution

We have $\overrightarrow{AB}=(4,1)$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}=(1,5)$. Then
$$\text{Area}=\frac12\left|\begin{vmatrix}4&1\\1&5\end{vmatrix}\right|=\frac12|20-1|=\frac{19}2.$$

## Solution

MISSISSIPPI has $11$ letters: $1$ M, $4$ I, $4$ S and $2$ P.

If all letters were different there would be $11!$ arrangements. Swapping identical letters does not change the word, so divide by the number of orderings of each repeated letter:
$$\frac{11!}{1!\,4!\,4!\,2!}=\frac{39\,916\,800}{1152}=34\,650.$$

## Statement

Prove that the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^2}$ converges.

## Hint

For $n\ge2$, compare $\frac1{n^2}$ with $\frac1{n(n-1)}=\frac1{n-1}-\frac1n$.

## Solution

For $n\ge2$ we have $n^2\ge n(n-1)$, so
$$\frac1{n^2}\le\frac1{n(n-1)}=\frac1{n-1}-\frac1n.$$

Let $S_N=\sum_{n=1}^N\frac1{n^2}$. For $N\ge2$, the sum telescopes:
$$S_N\le1+\sum_{n=2}^N\left(\frac1{n-1}-\frac1n\right)=1+1-\frac1N<2.$$

The sequence $(S_N)$ is increasing (the terms are positive) and bounded above by $2$, so it converges. Hence the series converges.

## Statement

Compute the distance from the point $M(1,2)$ to the line $\mathcal D:\ 3x+4y-5=0$.

## Hint

The vector $\vec n=(3,4)$ is normal to $\mathcal D$. Project $M$ onto $\mathcal D$ along $\vec n$.

## Solution

The foot $H$ of the perpendicular from $M$ has the form $H=M-t\,\vec n$. Requiring $H\in\mathcal D$:
$$3(1-3t)+4(2-4t)-5=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad6-25t=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t=\frac6{25}.$$

Then $MH=|t|\,\|\vec n\|=\dfrac6{25}\cdot5=\dfrac65$.

This agrees with the general formula $d=\dfrac{|ax_0+by_0+c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}=\dfrac{|3+8-5|}{5}=\dfrac65$.

@@@ divergence-of-the-harmonic-series.md

title: Divergence of the harmonic series

level: l1
difficulty: 3
tags: series, divergence
date: 2026-10-31
---

## Statement

Let $H_n=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1k$. Show that $H_n\to+\infty$.

## Hint

Compare $H_{2^{k+1}}$ and $H_{2^k}$: the terms between them are all at least $\frac1{2^{k+1}}$.

## Solution

For $k\ge0$,
$$H_{2^{k+1}}-H_{2^k}=\sum_{j=2^k+1}^{2^{k+1}}\frac1j\ge2^k\cdot\frac1{2^{k+1}}=\frac12,$$
because the sum has $2^k$ terms, each at least $\frac1{2^{k+1}}$.

Adding these inequalities for $0,1,\dots,k-1$ and using $H_1=1$ gives
$$H_{2^k}\ge1+\frac k2.$$

The sequence $(H_n)$ is increasing and has a subsequence going to $+\infty$, so it is unbounded and $H_n\to+\infty$.

## Statement

Prove that $e^x\ge1+x$ for every real $x$, with equality only for $x=0$.

## Hint

Study the function $f(x)=e^x-1-x$.

## Solution

Let $f(x)=e^x-1-x$. Then $f'(x)=e^x-1$.

- For $x<0$ we have $e^x<1$, so $f'(x)<0$ and $f$ is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty,0]$.
- For $x>0$ we have $e^x>1$, so $f'(x)>0$ and $f$ is strictly increasing on $[0,+\infty)$.

So $f$ has a strict minimum at $0$, where $f(0)=0$. Therefore $f(x)>0$ for $x\ne0$, which gives $e^x\ge1+x$ with equality only at $x=0$.

## Statement

A right triangle has legs $a$ and $b$ and hypotenuse $c$. Show that the radius of its inscribed circle is $r=\dfrac{a+b-c}{2}$. What is $r$ for the $3$-$4$-$5$ triangle?

## Hint

Use the fact that the two tangent segments from a vertex to a circle have equal length.

## Solution

Let the right angle be at $C$, with $CB=a$ and $CA=b$. The incircle touches the legs at two points. Together with $C$ and the centre $I$, these points form a square of side $r$ (all four angles are right angles and two adjacent sides equal $r$). So the tangent length from $C$ is $r$.

Hence the tangent length from $B$ is $a-r$ and from $A$ is $b-r$. The hypotenuse $AB$ is split by its point of tangency into these two lengths, so
$$(a-r)+(b-r)=c\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=\frac{a+b-c}{2}.$$

For the $3$-$4$-$5$ triangle, $r=\dfrac{3+4-5}{2}=1$.

## Statement

Find the radius of convergence of the power series $\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{n}{2^n}x^n$, and study its behaviour at the endpoints of the interval of convergence.

## Hint

Use d'Alembert's ratio test on $a_n=\dfrac n{2^n}$.

## Solution

Let $a_n=\dfrac n{2^n}$. Then
$$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{n+1}{2n}\longrightarrow\frac12,$$
so the radius of convergence is $R=2$.

At $x=\pm2$ the general term is $n\,(\pm1)^n$, whose absolute value $n$ does not tend to $0$. The series diverges at both endpoints, so the interval of convergence is the open interval $(-2,2)$.


@@@ sine-is-1-lipschitz.md

## Statement

Prove that for all real numbers $a$ and $b$,
$$|\sin b-\sin a|\le|b-a|.$$

## Hint

Apply the Mean Value Theorem to $\sin$ on the interval between $a$ and $b$.

## Solution

If $a=b$ both sides are $0$. Otherwise assume $a<b$ (the other case is symmetric). The function $\sin$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, with derivative $\cos$.

By the Mean Value Theorem there is $c\in(a,b)$ such that
$$\sin b-\sin a=\cos c\,(b-a).$$

Since $|\cos c|\le1$, we get $|\sin b-\sin a|=|\cos c|\,|b-a|\le|b-a|$.

category: geometry/plane
level: lycee
difficulty: 2

date: 2026-10-31

## Statement

Let $[AB]$ be a diameter of a circle with centre $O$, and let $M$ be a point of the circle different from $A$ and $B$. Prove that the angle $\widehat{AMB}$ is a right angle.

## Hint

Triangles $OAM$ and $OBM$ are isosceles.

## Solution

Since $OA=OM=OB$, the triangles $OAM$ and $OBM$ are isosceles at $O$. Let $\alpha=\widehat{OAM}=\widehat{OMA}$ and $\beta=\widehat{OBM}=\widehat{OMB}$.

The angle $\widehat{AMB}$ equals $\widehat{OMA}+\widehat{OMB}=\alpha+\beta$.

The angles of triangle $ABM$ are $\alpha$ (at $A$), $\beta$ (at $B$) and $\alpha+\beta$ (at $M$). Their sum is $180^\circ$, so $2(\alpha+\beta)=180^\circ$, hence $\widehat{AMB}=\alpha+\beta=90^\circ$.

@@@ the-centroid-of-a-triangle.md

title: The centroid of a triangle
category: geometry/plane
level: lycee
difficulty: 3
tags: vectors, medians
date: 2026-10-31

## Statement

In a triangle $ABC$, show that the three medians meet at one point $G$, and that $G$ lies at two thirds of each median from its vertex.

## Hint

Work with position vectors and look at the point $G=\frac13(A+B+C)$.

## Solution

Identify points with their position vectors. Let $M=\frac12(B+C)$ be the midpoint of $BC$, so $AM$ is the median from $A$.

Define $G=\dfrac{A+B+C}3$. Then
$$A+\frac23(M-A)=\frac A3+\frac{2M}3=\frac A3+\frac{B+C}3=G.$$
So $G$ lies on the median $AM$, at two thirds of the way from $A$ to $M$.

The expression $\frac{A+B+C}3$ is symmetric in $A,B,C$, so the same computation shows $G$ lies on the other two medians, also at two thirds from the vertex. Hence the three medians are concurrent at $G$.

@@@ the-logarithm-lies-below-the-line.md

level: l1

date: 2026-10-31

## Statement

Prove that $\ln(1+x)\le x$ for every $x>-1$.

## Hint

Study $f(x)=x-\ln(1+x)$ on $(-1,+\infty)$.

## Solution

Let $f(x)=x-\ln(1+x)$ for $x>-1$. Then
$$f'(x)=1-\frac1{1+x}=\frac{x}{1+x}.$$

Since $1+x>0$, $f'(x)<0$ on $(-1,0)$ and $f'(x)>0$ on $(0,+\infty)$. So $f$ has its minimum at $0$, where $f(0)=0$. Hence $f(x)\ge0$, that is $\ln(1+x)\le x$.
